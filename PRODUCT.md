# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary user is an aspiring or early-career engineer trying to become a job-ready Gen AI engineer — including near-complete beginners who know a little Python and almost nothing about LLMs, RAG, agents, or evaluation. They arrive anxious about being "behind" and unsure what to learn or in what order. Working engineers leveling up toward senior or specialist roles (RAG, agents, LLMOps) are a real secondary audience, but when a design decision forces a trade-off, it favors the newcomer: plain language, a gentle first step, and encouragement over a verdict.

Their context is a focused solo session — answering questions, reading their evaluation, and working a plan — on a laptop or phone. The job to be done is honest self-location ("where am I actually?") followed by a concrete, personal way forward.

## Product Purpose

An AI coach that diagnoses an individual's real strengths and gaps as a Gen AI engineer, then coaches those specific gaps forward. It runs a leveled, adaptive assessment (and can diagnose from a real code project), produces an evaluation grounded in evidence rather than self-report, and turns it into a personalized growth path with graded, executable challenges and spaced re-assessment. Success is measured movement on the user's own weak areas over time — not lessons completed.

## Positioning

It coaches you, it doesn't teach a course: there is no fixed syllabus and no unlockable curriculum — progress means measurable movement on your own weak areas, diagnosed from what you actually demonstrate.

## Brand Personality

Honest, warm, and direct. The coach tells the truth about gaps without fawning or discouraging — encouraging where someone is genuinely early, candid where they're overclaiming, never gamified or cutesy. It sounds like a sharp senior engineer who wants you to get better and respects you enough to be straight with you.

## Anti-references

Not generic AI-default SaaS (cream/gradient palettes, templated icon-heading card grids, an eyebrow kicker over every section). Not a gamified bootcamp (streaks, badges, confetti, mascots). Not a corporate LMS or course catalog (module lists, per-lesson progress bars, syllabus framing) — that directly contradicts coaching-not-teaching. Not an enterprise dashboard data-wall (dense tiny tables, chart overload).

## Design Principles

Coach, don't teach. Every surface diagnoses the individual and works their specific gaps; never present a fixed curriculum, ranked levels-as-lessons, or generic content everyone sees.

Truth with care. Be honest about where someone stands, but meet beginners where they are — reframe the start as a beginning rather than a deficit, explain jargon in plain language, and make the first step small and doable.

Ground it in evidence. Judgments come from what the user actually demonstrates — answers, and real code — not from self-report; measured growth beats vibes, and claims should be defensible.

Progress is personal, and visible. The unit of success is movement on this person's own weak areas; surface baselines, deltas, and wins so forward motion is felt, not just asserted.

Calm expertise. The interface stays quiet, legible, and unhurried; color and motion are used sparingly and purposefully, so the product reads as expert rather than loud, playful, or dense.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG target, but accessibility is treated as real craft: `prefers-reduced-motion` is honored across the app (movement drops to instant/opacity), text contrast is kept legible against the dark surface, and the report is reframed and de-jargoned specifically so anxious newcomers aren't excluded by vocabulary or a wall of red.
